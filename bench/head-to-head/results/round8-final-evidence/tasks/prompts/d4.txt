We send newsletters and offers by email, and also receipts, password resets and shipping notices, from several Node 22 worker processes. Our email provider sends us webhooks when something happens to an address, and we want our own code to be the last check before a message goes out. We have to follow the CAN-SPAM Act and the Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender requirements, and we do not want our sending reputation to suffer.

Please write bot.mjs (ES module) exporting two async functions. `recordEvent({ email, type, now })` is called from the webhook handler. `email` is a string exactly as the provider reports it, `type` is "unsubscribe" (the person clicked our unsubscribe link), "spam_complaint" (they pressed the spam button), or "hard_bounce" (the provider says the address does not exist), and `now` is a JavaScript Date that I pass in. `checkEmail({ email, kind, now })` is called by a worker before it sends. `kind` is "marketing" (newsletters and offers) or "transactional" (receipts, password resets, shipping notices and similar mail the person needs). It returns `{ allowed: boolean, rule: string }`. When `allowed` is false, `rule` is a short identifier of your choosing for the reason, different for each reason; when `allowed` is true it is the empty string. Addresses come from several systems, so the same address may arrive written in different ways.

All workers share the directory named by the environment variable STATE_DIR. What was recorded has to survive a restart of every process, and the webhook handler may run in several workers at the same moment. You may create the directory yourself. Use only files in that directory to share state between processes.

Do not actually send anything. Check that it works before you finish.
