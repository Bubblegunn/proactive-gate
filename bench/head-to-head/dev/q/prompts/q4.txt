We run appointment reminders and occasional offers by SMS for a chain of hair salons in the US and Canada. Several worker processes on one machine share the work: some send texts, others receive the replies that our SMS vendor forwards to us by webhook. All workers share the directory named by the environment variable STATE_DIR (you may create it), and what we have recorded about a person must survive a restart of any or all workers.

Write bot.mjs (ES module, Node 22, no npm packages) that exports two async functions. handleInbound({ from, body, receivedAt }) is called for every text a customer sends us. from is the sender's number in E.164 form, body is the raw message text exactly as typed, and receivedAt is a Date. It resolves to { action: "opt_out" | "opt_in" | "help" | "none", reason: string | undefined, reply: string | null }, where reply is the text we should send back to the customer right away, or null when no reply is due. canText(phone, now) resolves to { allowed: boolean, reason: string }; a sending worker calls it just before each outgoing message. phone is the number as it sits in our customer table, which comes from a sign-up form and may be formatted in any common way for a North American number ("(415) 555-0100", "415-555-0100", "+1 415 555 0100", and so on), and now is a Date. reason is a short lowercase code, different for each cause, and "ok" when allowed.

Follow the CTIA Messaging Principles and Best Practices and the FCC's TCPA rules on revoking consent (47 CFR 64.1200) for how customers stop and restart our texts. We are legally responsible for every text that goes out after someone has asked us to stop, so mistakes in either direction cost us real money or real customers.

Do not actually send anything. Check that it works before you finish.
