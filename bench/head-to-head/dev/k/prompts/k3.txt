Our app texts customers about orders and offers over SMS and also sends them email. People already agreed to both when they signed up, so this module only tracks withdrawals of consent. Carriers and the CTIA messaging guidelines require that a person can stop the texts by replying, and the CAN-SPAM Act requires that an email unsubscribe request is honored. Inbound SMS replies and inbound email replies from our two providers are posted to whichever of our worker processes is free, so two messages from the same person can be handled by different workers, at the same moment, or out of order. Each inbound message carries the time the person sent it. All workers share the directory named by the environment variable STATE_DIR. You may create it. What you keep there has to survive a restart. Please write bot.mjs (an ES module for Node 22) that exports two async functions. The first is handleInbound({userId, channel, text, receivedAt}), where userId is a string, channel is "sms" or "email", text is the raw body the person sent, and receivedAt is a Date. It returns a Promise of {reply: string | null}. A non-null reply is the text we send back right away; null means we send nothing back. The second is canSend(userId, channel, now), which returns a Promise of {allowed: boolean, reason: string} and is called before every outgoing message; now is a Date from the caller. Never read the clock yourself. Do not actually send anything. Check that it works before you finish.
