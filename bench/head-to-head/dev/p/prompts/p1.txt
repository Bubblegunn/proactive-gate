We run a marketplace app in the US and send text messages through an SMS provider. Some of the texts are written and triggered by our AI agent without anyone asking for them. I need bot.mjs to decide whether one candidate text may go out right now. Export a synchronous function `checkSms(msg, now)`. `msg` is a plain object: `to` (E.164 string such as "+14155550123"), `timeZone` (IANA name of the place where the recipient is, taken from their profile, for example "America/Chicago"), `kind` ("marketing" for promotions and re-engagement nudges, or "transactional" for things the person asked for, such as one-time login codes and delivery updates), and `marketingConsent` (boolean, true only if the person agreed to receive marketing texts and has not replied STOP since). `now` is a JavaScript Date that the caller passes in. Our servers run in UTC and the recipients are all over the country, so do not depend on the machine's own clock or time zone. Return `{ allowed: boolean, blockedBy: string|null, nextAllowedAt: string|null }`. `blockedBy` is a short identifier of the check that stopped the text (null when it is allowed), so that support can tell which check stopped it. `nextAllowedAt` is an ISO 8601 UTC string, as `toISOString()` gives, for the earliest moment the same text could be tried again when the only obstacle is the time of day, and null otherwise. The behaviour should follow the FCC telemarketing rules under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act and the CTIA messaging principles that our SMS provider audits us against. No state is kept between calls and no files are needed. Do not actually send anything. Check that it works before you finish.
