We send email from a SaaS product: receipts, password resets and security notices ("transactional"), and product news plus AI-agent-written re-engagement emails ("marketing"). Several worker processes send in parallel. They share a directory named by the environment variable STATE_DIR (create it if it does not exist), and anything that has to be remembered must live there so that it survives restarts and is the same for all workers. Write bot.mjs exporting three async functions. `unsubscribe(email, now)` is called when a person clicks the unsubscribe link or replies asking us to stop. `recordBounce(email, type, now)` is called from our delivery webhook; `type` is "hard" (the mailbox does not exist or the domain rejects us for good) or "soft" (mailbox full, temporary failure). `mayEmail({ email, kind, now })` is called before every send; `kind` is "marketing" or "transactional". Addresses arrive as typed by users or as stored by upstream systems, so they may differ in capitals or have stray spaces around them. All three take `now` as a Date. `mayEmail` returns `{ allowed: boolean, blockedBy: string|null }`, where `blockedBy` is a short identifier of the reason the email was stopped (null when allowed), because our deliverability team needs to know why a given address was skipped. The functions are called concurrently from many workers. We are a US company bound by the CAN-SPAM Act, and our email provider's bounce-handling policy says that repeatedly sending to dead addresses damages the sending reputation and can get the account suspended. Do not actually send anything. Check that it works before you finish.
